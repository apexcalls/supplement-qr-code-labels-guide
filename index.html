Why Supplement Brands Print QR Codes on Labels

If you have bought a supplement recently, you may have noticed a small square barcode printed on the label: a QR code. Brands are adding these codes for a reason. A static label can only hold so much information, whereas a QR code can connect your phone directly to detailed, up-to-date product information that would never fit on a tub or bottle.

So what do brands actually link these codes to? One common destination is the Certificate of Analysis (CoA) for your specific batch. A CoA is a laboratory report confirming what is in the product and that it has passed tests for contaminants such as heavy metals or microbes. Some brands also use QR codes for batch traceability, letting you check the manufacturing date and batch number against the one printed on your container. Others link to brand stories that explain where ingredients are sourced, or to usage videos that show how to mix or dose the product correctly.

These links are useful, but only if you verify that the QR code leads to a genuine page. Start by checking the domain before you tap: does the address belong to the brand itself, or to a recognised third-party laboratory? Look for batch-specific data on the page you land on, such as a CoA that matches the batch number on your label. A genuine verification page usually names the product, the batch and the testing dates.

There are red flags to watch for as well. Some codes lead nowhere at all, opening a blank page or an expired link, which tells you nothing about what is inside the tub. Others link to a generic homepage rather than anything product-specific; a homepage cannot verify a particular batch. Reprinted labels are another risk: counterfeit products can copy a genuine QR code, so a working code on its own is not proof of authenticity. Be cautious of codes that ask for personal details or direct you to download an unfamiliar app, as these may be phishing attempts.

QR codes are best treated as a starting point, not the whole story. Combine them with other checks, such as the guidance in its label-checking guide and the detailed breakdown in a GLPro formula review, to build a fuller picture. Independent information from sources like thesupplementrating.com can help you judge whether a brand's transparency claims hold up.
